title: Lifelog age gap and healthy aging

Consumer wearables record sleep, activity, and heart rate continuously and at population scale, yet these lifelogs are rarely used as a measure of how fast someone is aging. This project builds a **lifelog age** model from wearable data collected by Samsung Galaxy Watch and by Fitbit devices in the *All of Us* Research Program, and defines the **lifelog age gap** as the difference between predicted lifelog age and chronological age.

The age gap is then examined as a phenotype in its own right. Genomics, proteomics, and microbiomics are combined with clinical laboratory measures and survey responses in a multi-modal design, and a phenome-wide association study (PheWAS) maps which conditions and traits accompany accelerated or decelerated aging.

The aim is to establish whether everyday wearable behavior carries a reproducible signal of healthy aging, and to identify the molecular and clinical processes that signal reflects.
